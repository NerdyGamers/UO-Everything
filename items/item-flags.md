# Item Flags

Item flags are the special markers that decide what happens to an item when you die, whether it can be repaired or traded, and whether it will eventually wear out for good. Check them before investing in any expensive piece.

## How it works

- **Blessed** — stays with you through death. Blessing comes from clothing bless deeds, certain virtue and quest rewards, and a handful of special items. A blessed item never drops to your corpse.
- **Insured** — the paid version of the same protection: pay the insurance fee (commonly cited around 600 gold per item, community recollection) and the item returns to your backpack on death instead of dropping. See [death and insurance](../mechanics/death-and-insurance.md).
- **Cursed** — cannot be insured or blessed; it drops on death regardless. High-end loot often carries this flag as a risk tradeoff for its power.
- **Brittle** — cannot be repaired and breaks permanently once its durability is exhausted (community recollection on the exact repair restrictions). Doom artifacts are the classic example: powerful, but with a lifespan.
- **Cannot Be Repaired** — a similar end state: when durability runs out, the item is gone for good.
- **No-trade** — cannot be given to another player; typically quest and event rewards, binding the item to the character that earned it.
- **Quest items** — tied to an active quest; usually cannot be dropped, traded, or sold while the quest is open.

A perfect weapon that is brittle has a lifespan, and a cursed artifact needs a death plan. Read the flags before you pay.

## Era notes

Item insurance arrived with Age of Shadows (2003). Brittle and no-trade flags came with later high-end content as balancing levers against permanent best-in-slot gear.
