# Spellbooks

Spellbooks are the mage's weapon: they hold the spells a caster has scribed and, in the modern game, carry properties and bonuses that shape spell damage output.

## How it works

A standard spellbook holds all 64 Magery spells once scribed. Beyond that baseline, several spellbook types matter:

- **Slayer spellbooks** — carry a slayer property (e.g., undead slayer, elemental slayer), adding bonus spell damage against the matching creature family. A slayer book is standard equipment for farming specific spawns.
- **Scrapper's Compendium** — a named spellbook with a package of desirable properties, a long-standing mage staple (community recollection on its exact stats; verify against a current reference).
- **Inscription bonus** — a spellbook scribed by a grandmaster scribe grants its caster a spell-damage-increase bonus tied to the caster's own [Inscription](../skills/magic/inscription.md) skill (community recollection commonly cites +1% SDI per 10 points). This makes a GM-scribed book meaningfully better than a store-bought one.
- **Magical spellbooks** — like other equipment, spellbooks can carry properties (FC, FCR, LMC, SDI) from [loot](loot-system.md), [runic crafting](../crafting/runic-tools.md), and [imbuing](../crafting/imbuing-system.md).

Spellbooks for the other spell schools work similarly: necromancer and paladin books hold their respective spell lists, and the Chivalry, Bushido, Ninjitsu, Spellweaving, and Mysticism books serve their skills (see [skills](../skills/README.md)).

## Era notes

Slayer spellbooks and the inscription SDI bonus are later refinements; the original spellbook was a simple spell container.
