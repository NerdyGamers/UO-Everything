# Player Economy

Ultima Online's economy is almost entirely player-driven: vendors, trade hubs, and auction culture move the gold, while NPCs play only a supporting role.

## How it works

The backbone is the **player vendor** system — vendors hired and stocked by players, standing in houses and at Luna's famous vendor mall, selling everything from crafted suits to [rares](../items/rares.md). Major trade hubs concentrate the market: **Luna** on most shards is the long-standing bazaar for high-end gear, while **Atlantic**'s population makes it the de facto cross-shard trading center. **Commodity deeds** let bulk goods (ingots, boards, regs) trade in stack form without the weight, and the rares market runs on reputation, provenance, and auction rather than fixed prices (see [Rares](../items/rares.md)).

Gold enters through monster loot, quest rewards, and BOD payouts; it leaves through vendor fees, insurance, NPC purchases, and gold sinks like house placement and addons. Over the eras, the money supply inflated enormously — what cost thousands in the early years costs millions now — driven by high-end farming, duping incidents in the game's history, and publishes that poured gold into the world faster than sinks removed it. Prices are therefore era-relative: veterans quote in millions where launch-era players quoted in thousands, and the most valuable items (server births, top artifacts) trade in amounts that would have been inconceivable in 1997.

## Era notes

Duping exploits and publish-era gold fountains shaped the inflation curve; each expansion's new sinks (insurance, addons, vet rewards) only partly offset it.
