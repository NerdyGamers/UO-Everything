# Getting Started

New players enter Ultima Online through the Young player system and the New Haven starter quests, which compress the early learning curve that used to take weeks.

## How it works

Fresh characters start with **Young** status, which grants accelerated skill gain, protection from player killing, and access to help channels for a limited time (or until renounced). The intended first stop is **New Haven**: the starter town's profession quest chains walk new characters through a template — warrior, mage, crafter, and others — handing out starter gear and pushing key skills toward 50 while teaching the basics of combat, crafting, and travel (see [New Haven quests](../quests/new-haven-quests.md)).

After the starter quests, the practical advice is consistent across eras: pick one simple template (a basic warrior or mage), train it in low-risk areas, and ask for help. The general chat and New Haven area are where veterans answer questions; [guilds](guilds.md) actively recruit new players, and many shards have new-player assistance programs run by players or EM staff. The [community knowledge sites](community-sites.md) cover everything the game doesn't explain — templates, keybinds, and which skills pair together.

Common early mistakes: spreading skill points across too many skills (the 700-point total cap punishes dabbling), venturing into Felucca unprepared, and buying expensive gear before understanding what a template needs.

## Era notes

The Young system and New Haven replaced the old sink-or-swim newbie experience; accelerated gains made the first days dramatically friendlier than the launch era.
