# Roleplaying

Ultima Online has sustained one of the longest-running roleplaying communities in online gaming: players who stay in character, build personas with histories and allegiances, and treat Britannia as a world rather than a game board. Taverns, courts, and guild halls are its venues.

## How it works

Roleplayers create characters with distinct personas — names, backstories, speech patterns, and in-world occupations — and interact as those characters rather than as players. RP hubs form around player-run taverns, theaters, and guild houses, where regular gatherings, in-character markets, and ceremonies take place. Some shards and communities are RP-focused by culture, with norms against out-of-character behavior in public spaces.

Roleplay coexists with UO's mechanics rather than replacing them: an RPer's blacksmith still forges with the [Blacksmithy](../skills/crafting/blacksmithy.md) skill, and an RP duel still uses the combat system — the difference is framing. Many roleplayers avoid or recontextualize mechanics that break character (insurance, resurrection as routine), and long-running RP storylines have produced shard histories as detailed as any official lore. Conflict RP — wars between in-character factions — blurs into the [guild](guilds.md) politics the game is famous for.

## Era notes

Roleplaying was central to UO's launch-era identity, when Origin marketed the game as a living world. It never had dedicated mechanical support beyond emotes and speech; everything the RP community built came from social tools like books, bulletin boards, and houses.
