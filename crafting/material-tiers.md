# Material Tiers

Beyond plain iron, leather, and wood, Britannia's crafters work with tiered materials — each step up the ladder adding resists, damage, or luck to the finished item. The tiers mirror the crafting skills in [skills](../skills/README.md).

## How it works

**Ingots** (mined and smelted, used by [Blacksmithy](../skills/crafting/blacksmithy.md)): Iron, Dull Copper, Shadow Iron, Copper, Bronze, Gold, Agapite, Verite, Valorite. **Leathers** (from skinned creatures, used by [Tailoring](../skills/crafting/tailoring.md)): Regular, Spined, Horned, Barbed. **Boards** (lumberjacked, used by [Carpentry](../skills/crafting/carpentry.md), [Fletching](../skills/crafting/fletching.md), and [Tinkering](../skills/crafting/tinkering.md)): Regular, Oak, Ash, Yew, Heartwood, Bloodwood, Frostwood.

Armor crafted from higher-tier materials gains resists scaled to the tier; weapons gain damage increase. Higher tiers also carry luck bonuses and extra durability (luck on tiered materials was added in a later era — community recollection on the timing). Crafting with a tiered material requires skill well above the item's base requirement, and failures still consume the (much more expensive) materials.

Colored ore comes from high-end mining veins and ore elementals; colored boards are a rare lumberjacking result; spined, horned, and barbed leather come from progressively tougher creatures.

## Era notes

Colored ingots date to the earliest eras of the game. Barbed leather, heartwood, bloodwood, and frostwood arrived with later expansions, and luck bonuses on tiered materials were added later still.
