# II. The Lost Lands

**Canon**

For generations the scholars of Britannia believed their world complete. Every coast was charted, every dungeon delved, every ruin picked clean by adventurers. We were wrong, and the proof lay in the dark.

In the lightless bottoms of the deepest dungeons, explorers found them: great stone serpents, coiled and silent, older than any kingdom. The serpent pillars. And through them ran a passage — not to another shard, not to the void, but to a land no living cartographer had ever mapped. The Lost Lands: a vast continent cut off from Britannia since time immemorial, joined to our world only by those ancient stones.

The Lost Lands had their own cities, Delucia and Papua, their own dungeons and riches. They had their own peoples too, and older hatreds than ours: the Ophidians, serpent-folk of the wastes, and the Terathans, insectile warriors of the hive-lands, locked in a war so ancient that neither side remembered its cause. Adventurers poured through the pillars seeking the continent's wealth, and in time the wars of its native peoples became the wars of Britannia's explorers as well. There is a lesson in that, though I leave the drawing of it to the reader.

The discovery remade the world's idea of itself. Britannia was not the whole of the world — merely the part that had been found first. The scholars updated their maps, the merchants their routes, and the bards their songs. And somewhere in the dark, the old serpent stones kept their silence, as they had through every age before.
