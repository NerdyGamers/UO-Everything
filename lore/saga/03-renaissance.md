# III. The Sundering

**Canon**

In a single cataclysmic hour, the world was mirrored. I write this long after, and still the old folk speak of that day in lowered voices.

A second facet of the world came into being — Trammel, named for one of Britannia's two moons — a perfect reflection of the known world in every hill and harbor, but governed by gentler laws. In Trammel no hand could be raised against another without consent; it was a land where the peaceful might live unmolested. The original facet took the name of the other moon, Felucca, and remained as it had always been: wild, dangerous, and free.

The histories say the sundering was worked by great magic, dividing the world into a facet of order and a facet of chaos. Whatever the cause — and the Lycaeum's shelves hold a dozen theories, none proven — every Britannian soon knew which side of the mirror they stood on. What followed was not a war but a migration, the greatest movement of peoples since the founding. Crafters, merchants, and the peaceable crossed into Trammel. Warriors, thieves, and the red-handed killers who had once ruled the whole world unchallenged remained in Felucca, which became their proving ground.

In the shorthand of the age they were called the blues and the reds, and at last each had a world of their own. Whether the sundering healed Britannia's wounds or merely hid them is a question the scholars still debate over wine. I record the fact; the judgment I leave to wiser heads than mine.
