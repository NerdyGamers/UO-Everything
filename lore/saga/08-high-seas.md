# VIII. The Sea Remembers

**Canon**

For long years Britannia's seas were a quiet backwater. Fishermen worked the coasts, merchants ran their routes, and little troubled the waves beyond the odd storm. The old sailors will tell you those were the dull days, and they are lying — they were the blessed days, and everyone knew it but them.

The High Seas changed all of that. Great beasts stirred in the deep: leviathans rising to smash hulls with their bulk, sea serpents coiling out of the swell. And where beasts led, men followed — organized pirate crews began seizing merchant ships along the trade lanes, and the sea lanes that had been safe for generations became a gamble.

Britannia answered the way it always has: with ships. The shipwrights produced a new generation of vessels — swift sloops, sturdy frigates, great galleons — armed with cannons and crewed by adventurers willing to fight for the sea lanes. Captains learned a new art of war: maneuvering for the broadside, timing cannon volleys across rolling water, throwing grapnels and boarding enemy decks with steel in hand.

There is an old saying among sailors: the sea remembers every ship it ever took. In this age the sea had much to remember, and the taverns of every port town rang with the names of captains and krakens alike. Trade flowed again — at the price of constant vigilance. The peaceful days did not return. They never do. But Britannia has never been a nation to surrender its oceans, and it did not surrender them then.
