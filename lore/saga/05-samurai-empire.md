# V. The Eastern Wind

**Canon**

Across the eastern sea lie the Tokuno Islands — Isamu-Jima, Makoto-Jima, and Homare-Jima — and for long ages they might as well have been another world. Then their ships came to our harbors, and nothing was quite the same.

The Tokunoans brought an island empire of ancient samurai tradition, ruled from the city of Zento, and with them came the ways of the warrior: Bushido, the code of the samurai, and Ninjitsu, the shadow arts. Britannia's fighters, who had known only the sword and the spell, learned new disciplines — the focused strike, the honorable execution, the art of vanishing like smoke.

The isles held their own dangers, and the old books do not stint on them. The restless dead walked Tokuno's haunted places. The Oni — ogre-magi of terrible power — stalked the wilds, figures straight out of the yokai tales island mothers tell their children to keep them close at night. And in time a great trove surfaced among the islands: the Treasures of Tokuno, minor artifacts scattered by fate and war, which the wise gathered and bore to the museum curators for greater prizes still.

The eastern wind blew both ways. Our merchants learned Tokuno's markets; their samurai learned our roads. To this day a warrior may be seen in a Britannian tavern wearing a kasa and speaking of Honor with a Zento accent — and no one thinks it strange anymore. So do worlds grow wider: not by conquest, but by ships.
