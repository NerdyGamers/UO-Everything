# I. Of the Shattering

**Canon**

Before Britannia there was Sosaria, and before Sosaria's fall the world was whole. So the oldest books tell, and I set it down as they tell it, for I was not there and no living man was.

Sosaria's wholeness was bound to a jewel: the Gem of Immortality, which held all the world within it. The wizard Mondain, proud and hungry, seized the Gem and sought to make himself master of all that lived. Against him came a Stranger from another world, stepping through a moongate with nothing but courage and a borrowed sword. The Stranger slew Mondain — but in the struggle the Gem was shattered, and with it the world. Sosaria burst into shards beyond counting, each a world unto itself, scattered like leaves on a dark wind. Ours is but one of them. Remember this, for everything that follows flows from it.

Mondain's apprentice Minax took up his war and carried it across time itself, striking at the world in every age at once. The Stranger followed her through the years and slew her at the very dawn of time. Yet the two left behind a darker legacy: Exodus, their offspring, neither man nor machine wholly, a thing of malice and iron. A third time the Stranger came, and unmade it.

Then came the great turning. The Stranger returned once more, and this time the task was not slaughter but understanding: the Quest of the Avatar. Through the Eight Virtues — Honesty, Compassion, Valor, Justice, Sacrifice, Honor, Spirituality, Humility, born of Truth, Love, and Courage — the Stranger became the Avatar, the living embodiment of the good the world could be. From the lightless deep the Avatar raised the Codex of Ultimate Wisdom, and Lord British raised Britannia upon its teachings. The shrines were built. For a little while, the world was at peace.

It did not last. Three shards of the broken Gem took on a terrible life and became the Shadowlords — Hatred, Falsehood, and Cowardice — who seized Lord British himself and twisted his regent Blackthorn's rule into tyranny. The Avatar descended into the Underworld and freed the king, and the Shadowlords were banished. Then the gargoyles, a dispossessed people, stole the Codex in desperation, and there was war born of misunderstanding until the Avatar carried the Codex to the Shrine of Singularity and made peace between the peoples.

Last of the old evils came the Guardian, a great red presence from beyond the moons, working through the Fellowship's honeyed lies and a Black Gate of his making. The Avatar cast the Black Gate down — and was drawn through it into exile. Of the Guardian's end the old books grow quarrelsome, and I will not pretend to settle what sages dispute. This much is certain: the Avatar is gone from the world, and Britannia has stood without its champion ever since.

Here ends the tale of the beginnings. What follows is the history of our own age — the Britannia we know.
