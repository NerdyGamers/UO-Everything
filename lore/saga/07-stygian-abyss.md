# VII. The Abyss Stirs

**Canon**

The gargoyles are Britannia's dispossessed. Once dwellers of the surface world, they were driven into the underworld by ancient wars with humanity — wars in which, the old books admit, the fault was not all on one side. There, in the lightless deep, they built Ter Mur, a city clinging to the walls of the great chasm called the Stygian Abyss. For centuries the two peoples had no contact but old hatreds, nursed like embers.

The age of the Stygian Abyss opens, remarkably, with hope. The Gargoyle Queen reached out to the human kingdoms, seeking the peace her people have never known — and for a time, it seemed the ancient enmity might finally end. Envoys were exchanged. The winged folk walked openly in our cities, and our scholars walked in Ter Mur, marveling at a civilization built in defiance of the dark.

But the Abyss itself was stirring. In its lowest depths gathered Virtuebane, a demon of pure malice, and his shadow fell over both nations. Peace, it turned out, would have to be fought for. Champions descended into the Abyss — the greatest dungeon of the age, a vertical world of horrors — to face what waited at the bottom. What they found there, and what it cost them, the songs have not finished telling.

I write this with the outcome still uncertain, as all honest chronicles of the present must be written. The Queen's peace holds — for now. The Abyss waits — as always. Whether this age will be remembered as a reconciliation or merely a pause between wars is for a later scribe to decide.
