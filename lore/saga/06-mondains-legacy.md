# VI. The Elves Return

**Canon**

The elves are among the oldest peoples of the world, and for ages they kept to themselves in Heartwood, a living city grown within the deep woods. When they emerged at last, the age took its name from the oldest evil of all: Mondain's Legacy — for the shadow of the first great villain lay long across the land, and old corruptions were waking.

The elves' grief centered on the peerless: beings of terrible power, each a corrupted echo of something once noble or natural. A ki-rin twisted into horror. A sorceress drunk on dark magic. A shapeshifter whose true face no living soul has seen. These ancients could not be met by armies; they could only be confronted by small bands of champions bearing the right keys, in hidden lairs scattered across the land. Many brave souls went down into those dark places. Not all came back.

The elven return reopened old questions. If the elves had watched from their forests through all the ages of men — through Mondain, through the Shadowlords, through the Guardian — what did they know that we did not? The elves do not answer such questions directly. They answer with archery, with patience, and with very long memories.

This chronicler notes, with the fairness his office demands, that the age named for Mondain's legacy proved one thing above all: that evil, once loosed upon the world, does not end with the villain's death. It echoes. It finds new vessels. The wise do not celebrate the fall of a dark lord; they watch the shadows afterward.
