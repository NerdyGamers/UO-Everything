# Renaissance

**Canon**

The sundering of the world into twin facets — Trammel and Felucca — and the social realignment that followed.

## The story

In a single cataclysmic event, the world of Britannia was mirrored. A second facet — Trammel, named for one of Britannia's two moons — came into being as a reflection of the known world: identical in geography, but governed by gentler laws, where none could raise a hand against another without consent. The original facet took the name of the other moon, Felucca, and remained as it had always been — wild, dangerous, and free.

The official histories hold that the world was sundered by great magic, divided into a facet of order and a facet of chaos. Whatever the cause, every Britannian knew which side of the mirror they stood on. Trammel became the home of crafters, merchants, and the peaceful; Felucca remained the proving ground of warriors, thieves, and the player-killers who had once ruled the whole of the world unchallenged. The great social realignment of the age was not a war but a migration — and the reds and the blues, in the shorthand of the time, finally had their own worlds.

## Key figures and factions

- **The moons Trammel and Felucca** — namesakes of the twin facets; Britannia's moongates have always been tied to their cycles.
- **The reds and the blues** — the murderers of Felucca and the innocents of Trammel, in the shorthand of the age (see [Notoriety](../../mechanics/notoriety.md)).
