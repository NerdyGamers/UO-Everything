# High Seas — The Maritime Renaissance

**Canon**

With the oceans of Britannia grown perilous once more — leviathans rising from the deep, pirates prowling the trade lanes — the age of sail returned in force, and the shipwrights answered with cannon-armed vessels built for war.

## The story

For long years Britannia's seas were a quiet backwater: fishermen worked the coasts, merchants ran their routes, and little troubled the waves. The High Seas changed all of that. Great beasts stirred in the deep — leviathans and sea serpents rising to smash hulls — and organized pirate crews began seizing merchant ships along the trade lanes.

Britannia answered the way it always has: with ships. The shipwrights produced a new generation of vessels — swift sloops, sturdy frigates, great galleons — armed with cannons and crewed by adventurers willing to fight for the sea lanes. Captains learned the arts of naval warfare: maneuvering for the broadside, timing cannon volleys, boarding enemy decks.

The maritime renaissance also fed the land: the sea's strange bounty — rare fish, sunken treasure, the spoils of pirate holds — flowed back into the ports, and fishing became a profession of genuine adventure rather than quiet patience.

## Key figures and factions

- **The pirates of the Britannian seas** — organized crews who prey on merchant shipping; the sea's outlaws and, to some, its freest souls.
- **The leviathans** — the great beasts of the deep, the ultimate prey for the boldest captains.
- **Britannia's shipwrights and merchant houses** — the builders of the new fleets and the keepers of the trade lanes.
