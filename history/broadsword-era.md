# The Broadsword Era

Since 2014, Ultima Online has been developed and operated by Broadsword Online Games — a stewardship era defined less by sweeping reinvention than by preservation, steady publishes, and opening the game to new players.

## What happened

- **The transition (2014):** Broadsword, a studio founded by former Mythic developers, took over live operation and development of Ultima Online (and Dark Age of Camelot) from EA Mythic. The handoff was presented as continuity: the same developers, a new home focused on live games.
- **Endless Journey (2018):** UO's free-to-play program. Endless Journey accounts can play indefinitely with restrictions (skill caps, housing, and veteran-reward limits among them), giving new and returning players a no-commitment way in.
- **The publish cadence:** Broadsword settled into regular numbered publishes — roughly monthly to quarterly — each typically bundling bug fixes, balance tuning, seasonal events, and the occasional new system or dungeon. Publish numbers climbed well past 100.
- **Anniversaries and preservation:** the 25th anniversary (2022) was marked with in-game celebrations and retrospectives, and the studio has leaned into the game's legacy status: veteran rewards, classic-content revivals, and quality-of-life modernization (such as the ongoing client work).
- **Fresh starts:** a seasonal fresh-start ruleset, New Legacy, was introduced for players wanting a from-scratch shard experience (community-documented; details continue to evolve).

## Lasting impact

The Broadsword era is the reason the game still runs: stable stewardship, a low barrier to entry through Endless Journey, and a publish rhythm that keeps the world maintained without breaking what decades of players built. For a game of UO's age, continuity *is* the feature.
