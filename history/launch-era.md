# The Launch Era (1997–1999)

Ultima Online launched on September 24, 1997 — the first massively multiplayer online RPG to reach a mass audience, and the game that defined the persistent-world genre.

## What happened

- **Launch and the land rush.** Players flooded in faster than anyone expected. Shards filled, housing spots were claimed within sight of towns, and the early economy ran almost entirely on player vendors and crafted goods from day one.
- **The assassination of Lord British.** As the story is commonly told, during a pre-launch stress test Richard Garriott's avatar Lord British was addressing a crowd of players when his invulnerability was accidentally left off — and a player named Rainz killed him with a fire field spell. Garriott reportedly took it in stride, and the incident became the most famous anecdote in MMO history.
- **Unrestricted PvP.** Anyone could attack anyone outside guard zones. Player killers roamed the wilderness, thieves lifted from the careless, and the [notoriety system](../mechanics/notoriety.md) — with its Dread Lord titles for the worst murderers — governed daily life. Every trip outside town carried real risk.
- **Explosive growth.** Subscriptions climbed steeply through the first year, with community recollection putting early milestones around 100,000 accounts — unprecedented for an online game at the time.

## Lasting impact

The launch era set UO's identity: a sandbox with real consequences, player-driven everything, and emergent stories no designer scripted. The notoriety system, guard zones, the shard structure, and the player-run economy all date to this era — and the tension between danger and safety it created drove every major design decision that followed.
